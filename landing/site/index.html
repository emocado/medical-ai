The problem

A report full of numbers. A patient full of questions.

Clinics hand over results written for other clinicians. Older patients go home unsure whether a number is fine, getting worse, or a reason to call someone today.

HealthMate saysYour kidneys are filtering a little less well than expected. This is not an emergency. Ask your doctor about it at your next visit.

Jargon and abbreviations

HbA1c, eGFR, LDL. Flags like H, L and HH. Without help, it is hard to tell a small change from a serious one.

Many medicines, many doctors

Pills from a GP, a specialist and a pharmacy, each with a different label. Dangerous combinations slip through.

The wrong language

Reports come in clinical English. Many grandparents read Mandarin, Tamil or Malay best, and rely on busy children to explain.

How it works

Photograph it. HealthMate explains it.

Each screen below is recorded from the app using its built-in sample files. Text is large, buttons are big, and every screen is in four languages.

Reports

Every result, with the lab’s own normal range.

Upload a photo or PDF of a blood test or discharge summary. You get each marker with its printed range, and a short summary you can switch between four languages or listen to.

  • A result with no flag or range shows “Ask doctor”. It is never shown as normal.
  • Read-aloud, plus follow-up questions in a chat that already knows the report.
  • Drug names, numbers and units stay exactly as printed in every language.

Urgent results

A dangerous number is never hidden in gentle words.

When a result crosses a fixed safety limit, or the lab marks it critical, a red banner appears on the home screen and above the summary. It tells you to contact your clinic today and lists emergency signs.

  • The rules are written in code, so they work even if the AI misreads the tone.
  • One large button calls 999.
  • The flagged values are listed so you can read them out over the phone.

Medicines

Scan the label. Know what each pill is for.

Point the camera at a pharmacy label or box. HealthMate copies the dose exactly as printed and checks the new medicine against everything you already take.

  • Doses come from the label only. When it isn’t sure what a medicine is, it says “check with your pharmacist”.
  • Well-known dangerous pairs, such as clarithromycin with a statin, are flagged straight away.
  • A daily checklist for morning, afternoon, evening and night.

Timeline

See whether things are getting better.

Every saved report goes on one timeline, ordered by the date printed on the report. Pick a test to see it charted over time, with the normal range shaded.

  • “Better” or “worse” is worked out from the lab’s numbers and flags. The AI only writes the explanation.
  • Tests are matched even when two labs name them differently.
  • Download a backup file and restore it on a new phone.

Voice and meals

Ask out loud, about the food you actually eat.

Tap once and talk. HealthMate waits for you to pause, answers aloud, then listens again. Ask about nasi lemak, roti canai or chicken rice and get advice that fits your latest results and medicines.

  • No typing needed. Tap any time to interrupt.
  • Spoken questions and answers are saved in the chat to re-read later.
  • If a meal can’t be scored, it says “Not scored” rather than inventing a number.

For my doctor

Walk into the appointment with one clear page.

HealthMate builds a one-page summary from your saved records: medicines with their label doses, results outside the range, what changed since last time, and the questions you want to ask.

  • Print it, show it on screen, or send it to family through the phone’s share menu.
  • It can be in English for the doctor while the app stays in your language.
  • Built only from stored records, never from a fresh AI guess.

Safety

Four things HealthMate will not do.

An AI that sounds calm about a dangerous result is worse than no AI at all. These limits are built into the app, outside the AI.

Call an unknown result normal

If the report gives no flag or range, the result is marked “Ask doctor”. Green is only shown when the lab’s own range says so.

Invent a dose

Doses are copied from the pharmacy label. If no dose can be read, none is made up, and the doctor summary says “dose not recorded”.

Hide an urgent result

Fixed limits trigger a same-day alert no matter how the summary is worded. The alert sits above everything else on the screen.

Pretend to be your doctor

The guide, Dr. Aisha, introduces herself as an AI helper. A medical disclaimer appears in the reader’s language.

Privacy

Your records stay on your phone.

There is no account to create and no central database. Reports, photos, medicines and dose history are stored in the phone’s browser, and backups are files you keep.

Kept on your device

Everything you save

  • Reports, photos and their summaries
  • Your medicine list and daily dose ticks
  • Meals, chats and your own notes
  • Backup files, which only you hold

Sent only when you ask

The item being explained

  • When you analyse a report, photo, meal, question or voice clip, that item goes through HealthMate’s server to the AI model, with the context it needs.
  • The AI key stays on the server and never reaches your phone.
  • Requests are rate-limited and size-capped.

Why it matters

Clear answers, in your language, with a safe next step.

HealthMate does not replace your doctor. It helps you understand what the doctor wrote, remember what to take, and know when to pick up the phone.

  • For patientsUnderstand your own results without waiting for someone to explain.
  • For familiesHelp a parent from far away with one shared summary.
  • For clinicsPatients who arrive with their medicines listed and their questions ready.

HealthMate is a research prototype and is not a medical device. It does not diagnose or treat any condition. Always follow your doctor’s and pharmacist’s advice. In an emergency in Malaysia, call 999.
